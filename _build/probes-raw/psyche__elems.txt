 BACKGROUND:
  Psyche is a NASA Discovery-class mission to map the surface and characterize
  the composition of '16 Psyche', an elongated 280-km diameter metal-rich 
  asteroid located in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. This 
  is the first mission to a metal-rich asteroid.

  Psyche was launched Oct 13, 2023 @ 14:19:43 UTC on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy 
  from Kennedy Space Center, LC-39A.  Stage separation occurred 62 minutes
  after launch at 15:21 UTC.

  A gravity-assist flyby of Mars will nominally occur May 15, 2026 @ 
  19:28:03 UTC at an altitude 4618 km above the surface (8008.4 km from 
  Mars center), over 252.368 W, 41.032 N

  The asteroid's gravity will capture the spacecraft into orbit in late July 
  2029 and the two-year prime mission will begin in August. Psyche will 
  successively lower its orbit over the span of a year, eventually coming 
  as close as 75-km from the asteroid's surface. 

  Staging orbits around 16 Psyche:

    Orbit A: Characterization 
     first ~46 days, 41 orbits (700 km alt., period 32.8 h, 90 deg. inclination)

    Orbit B (B1,B2): Topography & magnetic field
     B1: 92 days (190 orbits)  (303 km, 11.6 h,  90 deg.)
     B2: 100 days (206 orbits) (303 km, 11.6 h,  90 deg.)

    Orbit C: Gravity science & magnetic field
     100 days (333 orbits)     (190 km,  7.2 h,  90 deg.)

    Orbit D: Elemental mapping
     100 days (666 orbits)     ( 75 km,  3.6 h,  160 deg.)

 SPACECRAFT PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS:
  Dimensions (w/solar panels extended): 24.76 m long, 7.34 m wide
  Bus dimensions: 3.1 m x 2.4 m
  Thrusters: solar-electric ion (Xenon) propulsion, 
             Four SPT-140 Hall thrusters (900 W operation, 4.5 kW capacity)
  Solar panels: 20 kW at Earth, 2.3 kW at 16 Psyche
  Science payload: 30 kg

 SCIENCE INSTRUMENTS:
  - Psyche Multispectral Imager (2) 
     optical navigation, geologic, compositional, and topographic data
  - Psyche Gamma-ray and Neutron Spectrometer (boom mounted)
     detect, measure, and map Psyche's elemental composition
  - Psyche Magnetometer (boom mounted)
     detect and measure magnetic field
  - X-band Radio Science (2-m antenna system)
     measure Psyche's gravity field to high precision.
  - Deep Space Optical Communication (DSOC)
     test new laser communication technology for higher data rates than radio 
     receiver is at Palomar Observatory
     transmitter is at JPL Table Mountain

TIME

  Barycentric Dynamical Time ("TDB" or T_eph) output was requested. This
continuous coordinate time is equivalent to the relativistic proper time
of a clock at rest in a reference frame co-moving with the solar system
barycenter but outside the system's gravity well. It is the independent
variable in the solar system relativistic equations of motion.

  TDB runs at a uniform rate of one SI second per second and is independent
of irregularities in Earth's rotation.
